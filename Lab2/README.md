# Lab 2: Agile Backlog Creation & Sprint Simulation in Jira

**Project:** Housekeeping Ticketing System (HTS)
**Epics:** Ticket Creation & Routing, Ticket Lifecycle Management, Service Quality Feedback, Reporting & Administration
**Sprints:** Sprint 1 (5 stories, 13 points) and Sprint 2 (5 stories, 23 points), both completed

Screenshots of the backlog, story points, sprint boards and burndown charts are in the PDF in this folder.

## Reflection Questions

**1. Did your estimations reflect the actual effort?**
The story points show how complex each story is compared to the others. Admin Dashboard got 8 points because it pulls data from every other part of the system, while Complete Cleaning Task got 1 point because it is a single status change. Since this was a simulated sprint, the cards were moved to Done within minutes, so the estimates could not be checked against real development time. The ranking from easiest to hardest still made sense.

**2. Was your backlog well-prioritized?**
Mostly yes. The stories that matter most to users were marked High: Select Category & Location, Auto-Assign Technician, Auto-Escalate Overdue Tickets and Admin Dashboard. Sprint 1 took the Ticket Creation & Routing stories first, because nothing else works until tickets can be created. One Low-priority story, Complete Cleaning Task (1 point), also went into Sprint 1 because it is small and part of the same ticket flow. Looking back, a High-priority story like Auto-Escalate Overdue Tickets could have taken its place.

**3. How did your simulated sprint align with your plan?**
Both sprints finished everything that was planned, and nothing was carried over. Sprint 1 completed 13 points across 5 stories. Its burndown log shows some changes during the sprint: the stories were removed and added back once, and Update Ticket Status was reopened before being completed again. Sprint 2 completed 23 points across 5 stories with no changes after it started.

**4. What insights did the burndown chart give about your team's capacity?**
In both charts the red line drops almost straight down at the start of the sprint instead of following the grey guideline. This happened because all the work was completed within minutes in the simulation. In a real sprint this pattern would mean work is not being tracked as it happens, or the stories are too big to finish a little each day. The jumps up and down in Sprint 1's chart show the scope changes from question 3. The team completed 13 points in Sprint 1 and 23 in Sprint 2, which could be used as a starting estimate for planning the next sprint.
